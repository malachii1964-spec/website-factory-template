## Name & marks
- Brand name: **Chautauqua County Courier**
- Monogram: **CCC** — three interlocking C's (chain links / route loops / target),
  one uniform heavy stroke, one solid color, fully reversible, legible at 16px and
  at truck-door distance.
- Verbal shorthand: **"Triple-C" / "CCC"** — the reason nobody has to spell
  "Chautauqua." BUT never LEAD a phone greeting or radio spot with the acronym
  (it owns nothing in audio). Lead with the tagline + phone number; CCC is the
  visual/badge shorthand.

## Positioning (clarified — three sentences, not one run-on)
Chautauqua County Courier is the countywide on-call courier and errand service for
people and businesses who can't be everywhere at once. One local call gets almost
anything legal picked up, delivered, or handled — at one price we lock before we
go. Not an app that only moves food inside a few zip codes: one local dispatcher
who knows the county and owns the outcome.

## Category to own
Chautauqua County's on-call courier — one number for almost any pickup, delivery,
or errand, anywhere in the county.

## The moat (brand-critic's key strategic fix)
Differentiation vs. apps is real but COPYABLE by other locals. The true competitor
is DIY, informal favors, and other local operators (including a possible Pip's
relaunch). The defensible moat = a **named, accountable local dispatcher at the
center of the brand** + **breadth-under-one-vendor** (multi-stop, wait-and-return,
in-home trust, itemized receipts) that a rival must match capability-for-capability,
not just re-slogan. → "Same name, same number, every time" becomes the LEAD proof.
**OPEN — owner decision:** who is the named dispatcher / face of the brand?

## Tagline
**One call. Consider it done.**
Descriptor: *Countywide delivery, errands & concierge runs — one guaranteed price
before we go.*
Scope the promise at the point of the ask to kill over-promise tension:
*"Tell us what you need — if it's legal and safe, consider it done."* The price
guarantee attaches AFTER acceptance.

## Message hierarchy
**Primary promise:** Whatever you need moved, fetched, or handled in Chautauqua
County, one local call gets it done — at a price we quote and lock before we go.
- **Pillar 1 — A real local person, not an app.** [Named dispatcher], same name and
  number every time, knows the county, owns your job start to finish.
- **Pillar 2 — One guaranteed price, before we go.** Dispatch minimum + mileage +
  time + stops; you approve before we move. No meter, no surge, no surprise.
- **Pillar 3 — Almost anything legal, safe & insurable.** Multi-stop missions,
  wait-and-return, weekly senior routes, seasonal-home stocking, white-label
  business runs — the complex jobs the apps can't take.

**Objection-killers**
1. Hidden fees → "One guaranteed price before we go. You approve it first. The
   number we say is the number you pay."
2. Trust a stranger → "You deal with [named dispatcher], not rotating app drivers.
   We confirm every purchase, hand you every receipt, we're insured, and we're
   based in Westfield." *(print "insured" only once true)*
3. Business reliability → "We're your delivery department without the payroll —
   your brand on the doorstep, one point of contact, no vans to buy."

## Voice & tone
**Neighborly · Straight-talking · Squared-away.**
- DO: talk like a capable neighbor; lead with the price promise + the local person;
  use concrete county nouns (Westfield, Route 394, the lake, Jamestown); short
  sentences, real numbers, one clear next step ("Call or text").
- DON'T: app/hustle-speak ("seamless," "gig," "on-demand platform," "optimize");
  filler ("trusted partner," "one-stop shop," "committed to excellence"); over-
  promise ("we deliver anything," "instant"); surge/urgency gimmicks; hiding the
  human behind a form.
- Standardize on the phrase **"before we go"** (retire "before the wheels turn" and
  "before we lift a finger").
- Senior-facing material: lead with **"errands & delivery,"** not "courier."

## Brand architecture — branded house (one brand)
CCC is the only brand. The 8 services are feature lines in 3 buckets, never
sub-brands, never separate logos/numbers:
- **Everyday runs:** Local Delivery · Custom Errands · Multi-Stop Missions ·
  Wait-and-Return · Reservation Runs *(always tagged "lawful, non-restricted")*
- **Recurring care:** Senior & Family Routes · Seasonal-Home Concierge
- **For business:** Business Delivery Partner

## Logo direction
Three interlocking C's around a shared center (chain links / route loops / target).
One uniform heavy stroke; solid single color; fully reversible; no gradients/bevels
(they die below ~32px and look cheap on vinyl). Two lockups: monogram-only
(favicon/truck/hat) and monogram + wordmark (header/letterhead/van). Min monogram
~16px; clear space = height of one C. Must pass the truck-door test (readable at
40mph one lane over) AND the phone-favicon test.

## TRUTH GATES — do NOT print until literally true (owner + compliance)
- **"Insured"** — only after a commercial goods-for-hire / hired-&-non-owned auto +
  liability policy is bound, and you can name coverage on request.
- **"Guaranteed price"** — must be a written, honored contract term with a defined
  change-order rule, or it's a chargeback/complaint magnet.
- **Restricted items** (alcohol/tobacco/cannabis/Rx) — NY licensing + age-
  verification exposure; "lawful, non-restricted" enforced operationally, not just
  in copy. (Routed to compliance-advisor.)
- **Pip's closure** — say "filling the gap left when Pip's Delivery closed"; do not
  state the date or reason as fact until sourced.
