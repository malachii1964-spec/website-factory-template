# Source Policy

## Purpose

Rank evidence by fitness for a specific claim rather than by prestige alone.

## Source classes

### PRIMARY
Original authoritative material: statutes/regulations, official documentation, standards, source repositories, first-party filings, published datasets, court opinions, peer-reviewed original research, official product/API documentation, direct records.

Use primary evidence for load-bearing technical, legal, policy, specification, or capability claims whenever feasible.

### INDEPENDENT_SECONDARY
Independent analysis/reporting that verifies, interprets, or compares primary evidence. Prefer sources with transparent methodology, named authorship, correction practices, and direct links to underlying evidence.

### SPECIALIST
Industry or domain specialist material. Useful for implementation experience and niche context. Check incentives and methodology.

### COMMUNITY
Forums, Reddit, social posts, issue threads, testimonials. Valuable for failure discovery, user experience, and edge cases; weak for universal factual claims unless corroborated.

### USER_PROVIDED
Material supplied by the user. Authoritative for what the document says and often for user-specific state, but not automatically verified as externally true.

## Independence rule

Two articles copying the same press release are one evidence lineage, not two independent confirmations.

## Promotional-source rule

A vendor is primary evidence for its own documented interface, price, terms, and published claims. It is not independent evidence that its product is objectively superior.

## Temporal rule

For unstable facts, prefer evidence that is both authoritative and current. Record effective/event date separately from publication/access date when material.

## High-stakes rule

For legal, financial, health, security, or irreversible decisions, use authoritative sources and independent corroboration where feasible. Clearly distinguish informational research from professional advice when appropriate.
